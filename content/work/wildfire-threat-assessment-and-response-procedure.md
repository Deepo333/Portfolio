## Overview

The PSE Wildfire Threat Assessment and Response Procedure (PSE-WRM-003) sets out how Puget Sound Energy
watches for wildfires during the declared Wildfire Season, judges how serious a threat is to its
electric, generation, telecommunications, and gas infrastructure, and responds. It also sets up a
weekly Wildfire Situation Briefing every Monday, so leadership has a regular baseline of situational
awareness. I drafted it for the Wildfire Risk Management (WRM) program, working from subject matter
expert direction.

## The problem

During Wildfire Season, a fire near PSE equipment can call for anything from closer monitoring to a
wildfire operations planning call and company-wide notifications. Many groups take part, including
Electric Operations, Gas Operations, Emergency Management, Communications, Legal, and contractors, and
monitoring passes between teams from day to night. The procedure's job is to give all of them the same way to judge a threat, the same
triggers for action, and the same way to record what happened.

## My approach

- Set out who monitors and when: the on-duty Wildfire Liaison Manager on weekdays from 07:00 to 19:00,
  System Operations overnight and on weekends, with handoffs at 06:00 and 18:00.
- Stated the trigger plainly: any fire within about 2 miles of a PSE asset is evaluated, and fires
  farther away are assessed case by case.
- Defined three Potential Impact Levels (High, Moderate, and Low), each with its criteria in a callout
  box, plus a decision-precedence table for deciding which level applies.
- Made the responses cumulative, so each higher level adds to the actions of the level below it.
- Described the monitoring tools the team relies on (Watch Duty, Technosylva FireRisk, and PANO AI
  cameras) and the fields to complete in the incident tracking log.
- Included term and acronym tables, a RACI matrix, and a contact list appendix. Contact numbers are
  removed from this public copy.
